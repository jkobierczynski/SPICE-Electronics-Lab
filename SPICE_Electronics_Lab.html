Import a SPICE netlist

Paste a netlist, or choose a .cir, .net, .sp or .txt file. Files exported from this lab come back with their drawing. Other netlists are drawn as a ladder: one vertical rail per node, parts in a column.

Export as SPICE netlist

Standard SPICE syntax, written for ngspice and LTspice. The lines starting with *@ are comments that store the drawing, so this lab can restore the schematic exactly.

How the lab works

Drawing

  • Place a part: pick it in the rack, click on the sheet. R rotates, X and Y mirror. Hold Shift to place several.
  • Wire: drag from any pin, or press W and click point to point. Double-click or Esc ends a wire. A wire end that lands on another wire joins it; wires that only cross do not.
  • Edit: click a part to change its values on the right. Drag to move, Shift-drag on empty sheet to select an area, Del deletes, Ctrl+D duplicates, Ctrl+Z undoes.
  • View: drag empty sheet to pan, scroll to zoom.
  • Values: type 4.7k, 100n, 2.2u, 1M or 1meg. In these fields M is mega and m is milli. In SPICE files both mean milli, and the lab reads and writes files that way.

Measuring

  • Probe tool (P): click a wire or pin for that node's voltage, click a part for the current through it. On a transistor, click near the pin whose current you want. Click a probe again to remove it.
  • Current direction: I(part) is the current flowing in at the first pin: the + pin of a source, the anode of a diode, the left or top pin of a resistor as placed. For transistors and op-amps it is the current into the named pin.
  • Scope: scroll to zoom in time, drag to pan, double-click to see the whole run. The table measures what is on screen. Cursors give time and value differences.

What is simulated

  • Transient analysis: a DC operating point, then time steps with the trapezoidal rule (or Backward Euler), Newton iteration at every step.
  • R, C, L, independent V and I sources, diodes (with breakdown), bipolar transistors (Ebers-Moll), MOSFETs (level 1 square law), linear controlled sources E and G, switches, and an op-amp with finite gain, one pole and output rails.
  • Semiconductor models are the textbook equations with constant junction and gate capacitances. They leave out series resistance, charge storage and the Early effect, so switching edges are cleaner than in a full SPICE.

SPICE files

  • Read: R C L V I D Q M E G X, .model, .subckt (expanded), .param with plain numbers, .tran, and sources DC, SIN, PULSE, PWL, EXP, SFFM, AM. Anything else is listed after import and left out.
  • Written: square, triangle and sawtooth become PULSE; noise becomes PWL; the op-amp becomes a small subcircuit of standard parts. The AM source exists in ngspice but not in LTspice.
