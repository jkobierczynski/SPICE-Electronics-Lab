Import a SPICE netlist

Paste a netlist, or choose a .cir, .net, .sp or .txt file. Files exported from this lab come back with their drawing. Other netlists are drawn as a ladder: one vertical rail per node, parts in a column.

Standard SPICE syntax, written for ngspice and LTspice. The lines starting with *@ are comments that store the drawing, so this lab can restore the schematic exactly.

How the lab works

Drawing

  • Place a part: pick it in the rack, click on the sheet. R rotates, X and Y mirror. Hold Shift to place several.
  • Wire: drag from any pin, or press W and click point to point. Double-click or Esc ends a wire. A wire end that lands on another wire joins it; wires that only cross do not.
  • Edit: click a part to change its values on the right. Drag to move, Shift-drag on empty sheet to select an area, Del deletes, Ctrl+D duplicates, Ctrl+Z undoes.
  • View: drag empty sheet to pan, scroll to zoom.
  • Values: type 4.7k, 100n, 2.2u, 1M or 1meg. In these fields M is mega and m is milli. In SPICE files both mean milli, and the lab reads and writes files that way.

Measuring

  • Probe tool (P): click a wire or pin for that node's voltage, click a part for the current through it. On a transistor, click near the pin whose current you want. Click a probe again to remove it.
  • Current direction: I(part) is the current flowing in at the first pin: the + pin of a source, the anode of a diode, the left or top pin of a resistor as placed. For transistors and op-amps it is the current into the named pin.
  • Power: select a two-pin part and press Power for voltage across times current. Positive means the part takes power in.
  • Math traces: under the table, combine traces: difference, sum, product, ratio, derivative or integral.

Scope views

  • Time: scroll to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to see the whole run. The table measures what is on screen. Cursors give time and value differences. Traces with more than two different units get a lane each.
  • Spectrum: the frequency content (FFT) of the time span shown in the Time view. The table gives the fundamental, its amplitude, total harmonic distortion and the 2nd and 3rd harmonics. For a clean reading, zoom the Time view to the settled part of the signal first.
  • X-Y: every trace plotted against the one chosen as X. Use it for diode and transistor curves, hysteresis loops and Lissajous figures.
  • Bode: gain in decibels and phase against frequency, from an AC sweep of one source. It is a small-signal analysis: the circuit is linearised with every source resting at its offset, so clipping and switching do not show. A ratio math trace such as V(out) ÷ V(in) gives the gain between any two points.
  • DC: every trace against the value of one source, which is stepped from one value to another. Each point is a DC operating point: capacitors are open, inductors are shorted, the other sources rest at their offset. A d/dt math trace becomes the slope of the curve, which is the gain or the transconductance.

Parameter sweep

  • Sweep repeats the run for several values of one part (2 to 12) and draws the runs over each other, in every view. Choose the part, which of its values to step, the first and last value and the number of steps. Log spacing steps by equal ratios.
  • With one trace on screen each run gets its own colour. With several traces the chosen run is drawn strongly and the others faintly. Click a value above the table to read that run.
  • A sweep together with the DC view gives families of curves: collector current against collector voltage for several base currents, for example.

What is simulated

  • Transient analysis: a DC operating point, then time steps with the trapezoidal rule (or Backward Euler), Newton iteration at every step. The step is shortened wherever the estimated error of a step is too large, and points are added there; Fixed step turns that off and takes exactly one step per point.
  • AC analysis for the Bode view: the circuit linearised at its operating point and solved with complex numbers at 40 frequencies per decade. DC sweep for the DC view: one operating point per value, each found starting from the one before.
  • R, C, L, potentiometer, transformer, independent V and I sources, switches (by hand, timed, or voltage-controlled with hysteresis), diodes (with breakdown), bipolar transistors (Ebers-Moll), MOSFETs (level 1 square law), JFETs (Shichman-Hodges), linear controlled sources E and G, an op-amp and a comparator with finite gain, one pole and output limits, a behavioural 555 timer and logic gates.
  • Semiconductor models are the textbook equations with junction capacitances that follow the voltage, as in SPICE. They leave out series resistance, charge storage and the Early effect.
  • The timer and the switches have a state. The moment a state changes is located inside the time step, so their timing does not depend on the number of points. Logic gates are smooth: an inverter with a feedback resistor works as an amplifier.
  • The examples have been compared with ngspice, which runs the exported netlists and agrees closely; the README lists the result.

SPICE files

  • Read: R C L V I D Q M J S E G X, K between two inductors (a transformer), .model, .subckt (expanded), .param with plain numbers, .tran, .ac, .dc, and sources DC, SIN, PULSE, PWL, EXP, SFFM, AM with an optional AC value. Anything else is listed after import and left out.
  • Written: square, triangle and sawtooth become PULSE; noise becomes PWL; AM and FM become expression (B) sources, because SPICE programs disagree on the order of the AM and SFFM arguments. A potentiometer is two resistors, a transformer two inductors with a K line and two loss resistors, a timed switch a switch with its own pulse source. The op-amp, comparator, 555 timer and logic gates become small subcircuits; those of the timer and gates use expression sources, which ngspice and LTspice understand.
  • A parameter sweep is kept in the layout comments. Other programs ignore it.
